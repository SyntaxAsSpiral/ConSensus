# Patterns

Each pattern names a problem, the forces in it, and a resolution. It links up to the pattern it completes and down to the patterns that complete it. Adapt it. Do not stamp it.

Confidence: `**` seen many times and confirmed, `*` seen and plausible, no star a hunch.

## Large

### 1. A style per place **

The root. Every project lives inside it.

**Problem.** One house style makes every project a skin on the same thing.

**Forces.** Consistency is cheaper. Each project wants its own life. Shared conventions creep in through reuse.

**Therefore.** Give each project its own style, grown from its material and purpose. A shared convention is one tool, never a default.

**Completed by.** Fingerprint in the palette. Organic hosts. Medium from the material.

### 2. Fingerprint in the palette **

How a style per place stays recognizably his.

**Problem.** A project that leaves his usual taste can stop feeling like his.

**Therefore.** Let the medium be the project's own. Carry his signature as a small thread. Catppuccin accents are the usual thread, sepia-washed or dimmed as the medium needs.

**Completed by.** Many accents, one geometry.

### 3. Organic hosts *

The mesh and its machines.

**Problem.** Centrally themed machines feel interchangeable.

**Therefore.** Give each host its own mood, wallpaper, palette, and character. Keep the themes local and hand-tuned.

### 4. Medium from the material *

Serves a style per place.

**Problem.** A form picked by habit flattens the subject.

**Therefore.** Let the subject choose the form. A kitchen gets engravings. A dither study gets a live lab. A pipeline gets a tarot deck.

## Middle

### 5. Many accents, one geometry **

**Problem.** Many saturated colors at once turn chaotic. One accent alone feels thin.

**Therefore.** Use many accents, held in a strict, usually symmetric structure. Stained-glass leading is the model.

**Completed by.** Bindu.

### 6. Bindu **

**Problem.** A symmetric composition with no source feels like wallpaper.

**Therefore.** Put a seed at the center that the form unfolds from and returns to. An eye is one bindu. A jewel, a dot, a star, or a seed is also one. The eye is not required.

### 7. Names with lineage *

**Problem.** A generic name makes a thing interchangeable.

**Therefore.** Name it so the name carries the idea. Alchemical and mythic roots, ligatures, and old spellings are the usual means.

### 8. Print that remembers its process *

**Problem.** A digital surface feels weightless and unmade.

**Therefore.** Let the making show: dither, etching, tide lines, proof marks, crop marks. Honest traces, not costume.

**Completed by.** Hand-tinted engraving.

## Small

### 9. Hand-tinted engraving

Paused, pending an asset audit.

**Problem.** Etched line alone reads cold. Full color fights e-ink.

**Therefore.** A pale wash under the etched line, missing the line slightly, drying with a tide line, at L* 80 or above.

### 10. A living portrait

On hold. For Babette. Her look is loosely Belle-inspired and original.

**Problem.** A static avatar feels dead. Heavy animation breaks print and e-ink.

**Therefore.** An engraving whose hatching re-dithers at a few frames a second, with a handful of real gestures.

### 11. Under erasure *

Completes print that remembers its process.

**Problem.** Deleting the old version erases history. Keeping it in full clutters the page.

**Therefore.** Strike through what is true but outgrown, and keep it legible beside its successor. History, not typos.

### 12. One stack, one thumb *

Completes medium from the material. Sibling of under erasure. Both let the physical process show.

**Problem.** A scrolling list makes each item a row. Animating every card feels busy.

**Therefore.** Tie the stack to one scroll position. The middle card lies flat. Cards farther out bunch up, tilt, and shrink with distance. On release, the nearest card snaps to the middle. On glass, the full tilt. On e-ink, fixed steps, hairline edges, and no blur, shadow, or overshoot.
