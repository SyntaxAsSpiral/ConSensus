# Red flags

Screen every candidate before synthesis. A flag is a reason to revise or reject the shape.

Assume the next maker sees only what they opened, copies the nearest surface, and takes the shortest path that still reads as finished. Prefer the shape that hides more behind a smaller public face. Compare candidates on what that maker must hold, not on how easy the making was. A deep face is capability held behind a small surface. It is not a deep chain of layers.

## Shallow face

A large public surface with little held behind it.

- The person using it coordinates several moves for one act.
- Visible options expose internal stages.
- Learning the face does not save learning the guts.

## Leaked decision

One internal decision appears in more than one place, so a change has to be made twice. A wire, a storage shape, or a process stage showing on the face is leakage. Keep it behind the face.

## Order instead of ownership

The piece is organized by the sequence of making, or by the sequence of screens, instead of by the knowledge it owns. Parts that run at different times still belong together when they protect the same decision.

## Pass-through

A layer that forwards without adding a rule, an adaptation, or a distinct form. Remove it, or give the act to the part that can finish it.

## Split ownership

More than one place writes the same decision or keeps its own copy. Give each decision one owner. Others read it or ask.

## Two ways

Two ways to do one act. The next maker copies whichever is nearest, and both keep growing. Keep one. Remove the other in the same change.

## Reachable insides

The inside of the form can be used as if it were the face. The next maker will. Make the inside unreachable from outside, so the wrong reach fails.

## Hand-synced list

The same items live in two places, and adding one means editing both. Keep one list and derive the others. If a list cannot be derived, disagreement has to be visible.
